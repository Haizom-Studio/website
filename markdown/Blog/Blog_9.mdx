Connected products—**smart home**, **industrial sensors**, **medical peripherals**, **asset trackers**—share a common backbone: a device must **authenticate**, send **telemetry**, and sometimes receive **commands**. The details look different by industry, but the building blocks repeat. This article explains **transport choices**, **TLS**, **identity**, and **provisioning** without vendor lock-in jargon.

## Transport — MQTT vs HTTPS (and when both appear)

**MQTT** is a lightweight **publish/subscribe** protocol. It fits:

- Many devices reporting **small** payloads on variable networks,
- **Broker**-mediated routing (topics, ACLs),
- **Bidirectional** messaging with modest overhead.

**HTTPS** (REST over TLS) fits:

- **Request/response** patterns teams already use for web APIs,
- **Caching** and **CDN** patterns for device config,
- Tooling familiarity (OpenAPI, gateways).

Many products use **both**: MQTT for real-time telemetry and HTTPS for bulk config, firmware metadata, or admin APIs. The right split depends on **latency**, **firewall** rules, **broker ops**, and **team skills**.

## TLS — encryption is necessary but not sufficient

**TLS** protects data **in transit**. You still need:

- **Server certificate validation** on the device (correct root store or pinning strategy—pinning has **operational** costs).
- **Clock** sanity: certificate validation fails if the device thinks it is 1970—plan **NTP** or provisioning-time clock set.
- **Cipher** suites appropriate to your hardware (some MCUs lack hardware acceleration).

**TLS alone** does not prove **which device** is talking—that is **identity**.

## Device identity — stop using one password for every unit

A **shared secret** baked into firmware is trivially extracted from one device and then compromises **all** units. Better patterns:

- **Per-device keys** or **certificates** issued at manufacturing or first boot.
- **Provisioning** workflows that bind identity to your backend **without** exposing master secrets in images.

**X.509 client certificates** are common in enterprise IoT: mutual TLS gives strong authentication—at the cost of **PKI** operations (renewal, revocation, clock skew).

**JWT**-based device auth is also common: shorter-lived tokens, refresh flows, and server-side revocation—but the **bootstrap** problem remains: how the device first proves itself.

## Provisioning — factory vs user pairing

**Factory provisioning** writes credentials during manufacturing (secure programming stations, HSMs). **User pairing** (BLE, QR codes, app-assisted Wi‑Fi setup) happens in the field.

Each model has threats:

- **Factory**: supply-chain integrity, secure key injection, audit logs.
- **Field**: attacker-in-the-middle during pairing, user error, support fraud.

Document **threat model** explicitly: what you protect against, what you accept as out of scope.

## Backend and mobile alignment

If customers manage devices through a **web** or **mobile** app, **identity models** should align:

- Same **tenant** and **device** hierarchy concepts,
- **Revocation** that propagates to apps when a device is sold or stolen,
- **Audit** trails for admin actions.

Parallel ad-hoc auth between “device API” and “customer API” creates **security** holes and **support** pain.

## Observability and operations

Plan for:

- **Firmware update** channels (staged rollout, failure reporting),
- **Telemetry** volume growth (sampling, aggregation),
- **Rate limits** and **abuse** detection at the edge.

## Privacy and compliance

Collect **only** data you need; disclose **retention** and **purpose** in privacy policies. Regional rules (**GDPR**, **CCPA**, sector rules) affect **logs**, **analytics**, and **deletion** workflows.

## Summary

Cloud-connected hardware is a **systems** problem: pick transports that match constraints, use **TLS** correctly, treat **identity** as per-device, and design **provisioning** for your real threat model—not for demo-day convenience.
