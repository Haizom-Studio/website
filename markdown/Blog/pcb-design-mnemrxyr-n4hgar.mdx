If you have ordered PCBs before, you have seen the **impedance** column on the fab quote: 50 Ω, 90 Ω differential, **+/-10%**. **Controlled impedance** means the fabricator commits to a **process** that yields predictable **characteristic impedance** for specified trace geometries—so your high-speed nets behave as designed. This article explains the physics at a practical level, what you must give the fab, and how to avoid common layout mistakes.

## What “characteristic impedance” means on a trace

A PCB trace is not an ideal wire—it has **distributed** capacitance to nearby conductors and **inductance** along its length. At high enough frequencies, those effects dominate; energy propagates as a **wave** at a speed set by the dielectric.

**Characteristic impedance** Z0 describes the ratio of voltage to current **for a traveling wave** on that line—set by geometry (width, height to reference plane, dielectric constant) and material. When Z0 is **constant** along the route, waves propagate cleanly. When Z0 **changes** (neck-down, stub, via, connector), part of the wave **reflects**—superimposing on the signal at the receiver.

That is why **matching** matters: drivers, receivers, and sometimes **series** or **parallel** terminations are chosen so the **received** waveform stays within timing and voltage margins.

## Single-ended vs differential

**Single-ended** lines (one trace over a reference plane) often target **50 Ω** for RF-style interfaces or memory nets where the spec demands it.

**Differential pairs** (P/N) target a **differential impedance**—commonly **90 Ω** or **100 Ω** for USB, PCIe, and many serialized buses. The pair’s **spacing** and **width** together set both differential and common-mode behavior.

Always follow the **standard** or **IC vendor** reference—do not invent impedance targets from generic blog posts.

## Stack-up is not optional

Impedance control starts with a **stack-up**: which layers are **signal**, **plane**, **prepreg**, **core**, and the **thickness** and **Dk** (dielectric constant) of each dielectric.

Your **fabricator** provides approved stack-ups. You (or your SI engineer) map required Z0 to **trace width** and **gap** using:

- **2D field solvers** built into PCB tools, or
- **External** calculators—only if you confirm **reference planes** match the solver assumptions.

**Tolerance** matters: typical **+/-10%** on Z0 is common; tighter costs money. Budget **coupon** traces on the panel for measurement if the design is marginal.

## Where controlled impedance usually breaks

- **Via stubs**: unused barrel acts as an impedance discontinuity; **back-drill** or route to reduce stub length on very fast nets.
- **Layer transitions** without a nearby **return** path: stitch vias to keep reference continuity.
- **Neck-downs** into BGA escapes: match impedance through the escape region or accept **simulation**.
- **Connector** footprints: landing pattern and ground pins must match the reference layout.

## Differential pair routing habits

- **Length match** within **skew** budget from the spec—not “exactly equal” unless required.
- **Keep spacing** consistent; avoid **long** separations that break coupling assumptions.
- **Avoid** acute-angle bends where possible; use **arc** or **45°** mitered corners per tool best practice.

## Verification before fab

1. **Constraint manager** enforces width/gap/length per net class.
2. **DRC** passes for clearance **and** impedance regions.
3. **Fab** confirms stack-up and **impedance table** match their process.
4. Optional: **TDR** or **coupon** measurement on first article.

## When you can skip formal impedance control

Slow **GPIO**, **I2C** at short lengths, and **static** control lines on small boards may not need controlled impedance—**but** if you are near **edge rate** or **line length** limits, ask someone to sanity-check. Guessing costs more than a review.

## Summary

Controlled impedance ties **geometry**, **materials**, and **fabrication** together. Treat the **stack-up** as a contract with your fab, route **pairs** and **references** with discipline, and verify discontinuities on anything that runs fast enough to **close the eye** at the receiver.
